# Changelog

## 1.2.0 — 2026-11-26

- Plays better on phones: full screen on every phone, iPhone included, with a "Play full screen" button under the game.
- Sideways phones get their own layout: the stack sits beside the sheet at full height, and the Gazette opens in two columns, so nothing falls off the bottom.
- Tap a square on the sheet to pick the card printed on top there.
- Bigger buttons on touch screens, a gentle buzz when you move a card, stamp a row or press the sheet (Android, with sound on), and no more stray text selection or long-press menus.
- The Gazette and today's vote fit a small phone screen without scrolling.

## 1.1.0 — 2026-11-25

- After today's Small or Large sheet, tell us how hard it was with one tap, and see how your moves compare with everyone else's today. Anonymous: nothing about you is stored.
- A new "Tell us" card under the game: a thumbs up or down and a few one-tap notes (too hard, too easy, broke on my phone...), no account needed.
- A Privacy link in the footer.

## 1.0.0 — 2026-11-24

First release.

- Punched cards over a letter grid: turn them and restack them until every row reads a word, then press it into print.
- **Daily:** a Small and a Large sheet every day, the same for everyone. Monday is gentlest, Saturday trickiest, and Sunday's Large sheet has five cards. Streaks (print the Small sheet), a 14-day archive and a spoiler-free result to copy.
- **Journey:** 60 sheets in six chapters, from 3×3 with one card to 5×5 with four, including cards with a single lone hole and cards that look the same upside down.
- **Free play:** pick a size and how many cards.
- Every sheet has exactly one answer, proved by a solver that tries every stack. Par is the true fewest moves.
- Five printer robots, each with its own colour, tab pattern and face. Ink stamps for every row that reads, and the Oddvale Gazette prints your words as the headline.
- Tap, drag, mouse wheel or keyboard. Undo is allowed but counts as a move; hints make one good move for you.
- Word list: the public-domain ENABLE list for what counts as a word, with answer rows drawn from a hand-picked list of everyday words.
